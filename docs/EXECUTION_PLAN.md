## 1. The project in plain language

### 1.1 What problem are we solving?

Persons with disabilities (PwDs) in India — 2.68 crore people per Census 2011, 21 recognised disability types under the RPwD Act 2016 — are frequently **qualified for a job but filtered out at the conversation.** Not by the technical test. By the interview, the phone call, the daily standup, the moment they need to ask a supervisor to repeat an instruction, or the moment they need to request a workplace accommodation.

Training programmes that could fix this have two failures:

1. **They are not accessible.** They assume a user who can hear the audio, see the screen, speak with typical articulation, read at speed, and process one long instruction at a time. A Deaf learner, a non-verbal learner, a learner with cerebral palsy, and a learner with an intellectual disability are all effectively locked out of the same course.
2. **When they do use AI, the AI punishes the disability.** A standard pronunciation scorer compares your speech to a neurotypical native speaker. If you have dysarthria or a stammer, you will score badly forever, no matter how hard you work or how much you improve. The tool tells you every single day that you are broken. So you stop using it.

### 1.2 What are we building?

A **web application (installable PWA) plus companion trainer/institution dashboards** that:

1. Interviews the user at onboarding to build a **Communication Ability Profile** — which channels they can actually use to receive information (audio / captions / sign / pictures / simplified text) and to produce it (voice / typing / sign / symbol tapping / switch device).
2. Renders **every single lesson** through those channels. Not "accessibility options" bolted on — the lesson is stored as abstract data and only becomes a screen at the last possible moment, shaped by the profile.
3. Teaches a curated bank of **200+ workplace words and phrases** using spaced repetition.
4. Lets the learner **rehearse real conversations** — with an AI recruiter, an AI manager, an AI teammate — generated by an LLM that is grounded in that phrase bank and cannot go off-script.
5. Listens to their speech and gives feedback that is **measured against their own past performance**, never against a native speaker.
6. Runs a **full mock interview** whose scoring rubric is explicitly forbidden from grading anything that is a manifestation of disability.
7. Shows progress to the **learner**, to their **trainer / special educator** (who can override the AI), and to their **institution or employer** (anonymised, consent-gated).

### 1.3 The three ideas that make this defensible

Everything else in this document is engineering. These three are the product.

**Idea 1 — Accessibility as architecture (the "Modality Router").**
A lesson is not a screen. A lesson is a JSON object with a canonical meaning and a bundle of representations (native audio, slow audio, ISL video clip, pictograph sequence, Easy-Read paraphrase, phoneme string). A runtime *router* picks the representation. Consequence: **it is structurally impossible to ship a module that is inaccessible to a profile**, because the module author never chose a rendering. Compare to every competitor, where accessibility is a checkbox someone forgot to test.

**Idea 2 — Baseline-relative scoring (the "Personal Progress Index").**
`score ≠ similarity(you, native_speaker)`. Instead `score = f(you_today, your_own_rolling_baseline)`. The learner competes with yesterday's self. A stammering block is emitted as a *coaching cue* ("try an easy onset on the /p/"), never as a deduction. This is the ethical core, and it is also the retention mechanic — people keep using tools that show them winning.

**Idea 3 — The bias-guarded rubric.**
The mock-interview scorer has a hard-coded, version-controlled, auditable **exclusion list**: it may score content, structure, relevance, and self-advocacy; it is structurally prevented from scoring speech rate, articulation quality, gaze, facial affect, or motor stillness. Every score writes an audit record. We ship an automated **disfluency-invariance test** that proves the property holds (Section 10, M11). This directly answers the documented discrimination problem in AI hiring tools — and it is a claim we can *prove*, not just assert.

## 2. Glossary

Read this once; these terms are used without explanation later.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PwD** | Person with disability. |
| **CAP** | Communication Ability Profile — the per-user record of usable input/output channels, pace, and support needs. The single most important object in the system. |
| **Modality** | A channel of communication: speech, text, Indian Sign Language, AAC symbols, Easy-Read, haptics, switch input. |
| **Modality Router** | The runtime layer that turns modality-neutral content into a rendered experience for a given CAP. |
| **AAC** | Augmentative and Alternative Communication — e.g. tapping picture symbols to compose a sentence. |
| **ISL** | Indian Sign Language. |
| **Easy-Read** | A formal simplification standard: short sentences, one idea per line, common words, supporting images. |
| **ASR** | Automatic Speech Recognition (speech → text). |
| **Forced alignment** | Given audio *and* its known transcript, compute the exact time boundaries of each phoneme. |
| **GOP** | Goodness of Pronunciation — a per-phoneme confidence score derived from an acoustic model's posteriors, used for pronunciation assessment. |
| **PPI** | Personal Progress Index — our baseline-relative composite score. |
| **Prosody** | The music of speech: rate, rhythm, pitch, loudness, pausing. |
| **Disfluency** | Interruptions in speech flow: blocks, prolongations, repetitions, interjections. |
| **FSRS** | Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler — a modern, open, well-validated spaced-repetition algorithm. |
| **RAG** | Retrieval-Augmented Generation — grounding an LLM's answers in retrieved documents so it cannot invent content. |
| **ZPD** | Zone of Proximal Development — keep difficulty just above current ability. Our difficulty-adaptation target. |
| **STAR** | Situation-Task-Action-Result — the standard interview answer structure we coach. |
| **DPDP Act 2023** | India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act. Governs our consent and data-handling design. |
| **WCAG 2.2 AA** | The international web accessibility conformance target we hold ourselves to. |
| **IS 17802** | The Indian accessibility standard for ICT products. |

### 3.2 Explicitly out of scope (say this out loud, in the pitch, and be proud of it)

| Not building | Why | Substitute |
|---|---|---|
| A 3D real-time signing avatar | Linguistically hard (ISL grammar ≠ English word order); a bad avatar is worse than none and is offensive to Deaf users | Curated human-signed video clip library, plus a clearly-labelled `[V2]` avatar track |
| ASR trained from scratch | Requires thousands of GPU-hours and data we don't have | Adapt a pretrained Whisper/wav2vec2 — the adaptation is the novel part |
| Full ISL translation | Same reason as the avatar | Recognition of a bounded ~50–100 sign working vocabulary |
| Clinical diagnosis of any kind | We are a training tool, not a medical device. Regulatory landmine. | Explicit disclaimer; route concerns to the human trainer |
| Native iOS/Android apps at launch | PWA covers it; Flutter port is `[V2]` | Installable PWA with offline cache |
| Employer ATS integrations | No customers yet | Consent-gated export (PDF/JSON) |

### 3.3 The "no" that matters most
**We never store or transmit raw video, and we never store raw audio longer than the processing window.** Features are extracted, then the audio is purged unless the learner explicitly opts in to contribute to the research corpus. This is a hard architectural constraint, not a policy document. See M17.

### 5.2 Service boundaries and why they are drawn there

| Service | Responsibility | Why it is separate |
|---|---|---|
| **Client** | All rendering, all modality decisions, offline cache, on-device ASR fallback | The router must run client-side or offline mode is impossible |
| **API Gateway** | Auth, CRUD, orchestration, consent enforcement, rate limiting | Single security boundary. Nothing else talks to the DB directly. |
| **Speech Service** | Everything acoustic | Heavy Python ML deps (torch, MFA, openSMILE) that must not pollute the API's cold-start time. Different scaling profile. Deployed to a CPU/GPU host, not the web dyno. |
| **GenAI Service** | LLM calls, RAG, guardrails, rubric | Isolates the one component with an external paid dependency and non-deterministic output. Makes it independently testable and swappable. |
| **Learning Service** | Scheduling, recommendation, gamification | Pure logic, no ML deps, no external calls. Can live inside the API gateway initially and be split later. |

> **Decision:** Learning Service starts as a *module inside* the API gateway, not a separate deployment. Split it only if it becomes a bottleneck. Three deployments is the right number for a 2–3 person team; five is not.

### 5.3 The two contracts that everything depends on

If you get these two right, the team can work in parallel without blocking. Write them first (M0), version them, and treat changes as breaking.

**Contract A — `ContentBlock`** (what the Modality Router consumes)
```jsonc
{
  "id": "phrase.clarify.repeat_request_01",
  "kind": "phrase | scenario_turn | social_story_panel | interview_question | instruction",
  "canonical_text": "Could you please repeat that?",
  "intent": "request_clarification",
  "difficulty": 2,                      // 1-5, CEFR-mapped
  "scenario_tags": ["supervisor", "shopfloor", "first_week"],
  "representations": {
    "audio_native":  { "uri": "…", "duration_ms": 1420 },
    "audio_slow":    { "uri": "…", "duration_ms": 2380 },
    "isl_clip":      { "uri": "…", "gloss": "REPEAT PLEASE-YOU" },
    "pictographs":   [{ "set": "arasaac", "id": 2462, "label": "repeat" }],
    "easy_read":     "I did not hear.\nI ask again.\n\"Please say it again.\"",
    "phonemes":      "k ʊ d j uː p l iː z r ɪ ˈp iː t ð æ t",
    "caption":       "Could you please repeat that?"
  },
  "interaction": {
    "accepted_input_modes": ["speech", "text", "aac", "sign", "switch"],
    "target_response": { "type": "phrase_match", "ref": "phrase.clarify.repeat_request_01" },
    "hints": ["…"]
  },
  "a11y": { "requires_audio": false, "requires_vision": false, "requires_speech": false }
}
```
> The `a11y` flags are asserted in CI: **no ContentBlock may require a channel that any supported profile lacks, unless an equivalent representation exists.** This is the automated guarantee behind "accessibility as architecture".

**Contract B — `LearnerResponse`** (what every input modality normalises to)
```jsonc
{
  "session_id": "…",
  "block_id": "phrase.clarify.repeat_request_01",
  "input_mode": "speech | text | aac | sign | switch",
  "canonical_text": "could you please repeat that",   // normalised, comparable
  "raw": {                                             // mode-specific payload
    "audio_ref": "…",           // speech only
    "symbol_sequence": [2462],  // aac only
    "sign_predictions": [...]   // sign only
  },
  "timing": { "started_at": "…", "submitted_at": "…", "attempts": 1 },
  "self_report": { "confidence": 3 }
}
```
> **Why this matters:** because AAC, sign, typing, and speech all produce a `canonical_text`, the *same* scoring, the *same* recommendation engine, and the *same* dashboards work for all five personas. Only the speech-specific analytics (GOP, prosody) are conditional. This single decision removes ~60% of the work you would otherwise duplicate per modality.

## 6. The stack — chosen for zero cost and fast iteration

> **Verify current free-tier limits before committing**; these change. The point is the *shape*: managed Postgres + static frontend host + one container host + one ML host.

### 6.1 Chosen stack

| Layer | Choice | Free tier | Why this one |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frontend** | React 18 + TypeScript + Vite + Tailwind + Radix UI primitives | — | Radix gives accessible primitives (focus management, ARIA) for free. Do **not** hand-roll dialogs/menus. |
| **PWA** | `vite-plugin-pwa` (Workbox) + IndexedDB (`idb`) | — | Offline-first without a native build |
| **Frontend host** | Cloudflare Pages *or* Vercel | Generous, no card | Static + edge functions; instant preview per PR |
| **API + Learning** | FastAPI (Python 3.11) in Docker | Render / Fly.io / Railway free allowance | Same language as the ML side; Pydantic gives us schema-first contracts |
| **Speech Service** | FastAPI + PyTorch + ONNX, Docker | Hugging Face Spaces (free CPU, persistent) | HF Spaces will run a Docker container with 16GB RAM free, indefinitely. Best free home for CPU inference. |
| **GPU (training only)** | Google Colab / Kaggle notebooks | Free T4/P100 hours | Training runs are episodic; we do not need a standing GPU |
| **Burst GPU inference** `[V2]` | Modal or RunPod serverless | Small monthly credit | Only if on-demand ASR latency becomes unacceptable |
| **Database** | Supabase Postgres + `pgvector` | 500MB DB, 1GB storage | Postgres + vectors + auth + storage + row-level security in one free product. This is the single biggest cost saver. |
| **Auth** | Supabase Auth (email + magic link + anon) | Included | Anonymous sessions matter — a PwD learner should be able to try it without an account |
| **Object storage** | Supabase Storage → Cloudflare R2 when it grows | 1GB / 10GB | R2 has no egress fees; audio assets get large |
| **Queue / jobs** | `arq` on Upstash Redis (or Postgres-backed `pgboss`-style) | Free tier | Celery is heavier than we need for 2–3 people |
| **LLM** | Claude API (primary) · Groq/Gemini free tier (fallback + dev) | Pay-per-token, cheap | Abstract commits to Claude; keep the provider behind an interface (M9) |
| **Embeddings** | `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, self-hosted in Speech/GenAI svc | Free | 200 phrases is tiny; no need for a paid embedding API |
| **Monitoring** | Sentry (errors) + PostHog (product analytics, self-hostable) + Grafana Cloud (metrics) | Free tiers | |
| **CI/CD** | GitHub Actions | 2000 min/mo | Runs tests, `axe-core` a11y checks, contract validation, deploy |
| **Experiment tracking** | MLflow on a free host, or Weights & Biases free tier | Free | Required for the `[ML]` modules to be reproducible |

### 6.2 Cost ceiling
Everything above is free **except LLM tokens.** Budget: with caching, schema-constrained short outputs, and a small model for routing decisions, expect **well under ₹2,000/month during development.** Set a hard spend cap on the API key on day one (M0).

**Design notes worth defending in a review:**
- `speech_attempts.audio_ref_ttl` — the audio reference *expires*. A nightly job hard-deletes the object. Retention is a column, not a promise.
- `ppi_baselines` is a separate table with a rolling window, not a computed field, so the baseline is inspectable and auditable — a trainer must be able to see *why* a score moved.
- `rubric_scores.excluded_dimensions[]` is persisted **per score**, so an audit two years later can prove what the rubric refused to grade.

## 9. The ML / AI inventory — exactly what gets trained

This is the section people always want and never write clearly. Here it is.

### 9.1 The honest breakdown

| # | Component | Category | What we actually do | Data needed | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Base ASR** | *Use pretrained* | Whisper (`small`/`medium` cloud, `base`/`tiny` INT8 ONNX on-device). Zero training. | none | S |
| **B** | **Personalised ASR adaptation** `[ML]` | **Adapt** | 30-phrase enrolment per user → speaker embedding conditioning + LoRA adapter fine-tune on the user's own clips. Per-user adapter, ~2–5MB. | User's own enrolment (30 clips). Offline validation on TORGO / UASpeech / SEP-28k. | **L** |
| **C** | **Forced alignment** | *Use tool* | Montreal Forced Aligner (or `torchaudio` CTC segmentation as a lighter alternative) to get phoneme boundaries. | pronunciation lexicon | M |
| **D** | **GOP pronunciation scoring** `[ML]` | **Build algorithm on pretrained model** | wav2vec2/WavLM phoneme posteriors → per-phoneme GOP → phrase score. No training; the *math and calibration* are ours. | small calibration set | M |
| **E** | **Disfluency classifier** `[ML]` | **Train from scratch (small)** | Multi-label classifier over audio windows: block / prolongation / sound-repetition / word-repetition / interjection. wav2vec2 features → light head, or handcrafted openSMILE features → gradient boosting. | **SEP-28k** (~28k labelled clips) + **FluencyBank**. Both public. | **L** |

### 9.2 Summary answer to "is there model building?"

**Yes — three genuinely trained models and two novel algorithms:**

1. **Trained:** disfluency event classifier (E) — supervised, public data, real training loop.
2. **Trained:** ISL sign classifier (J) — supervised, bounded vocabulary.
3. **Trained/adapted:** per-user ASR adapters (B) — few-shot LoRA adaptation. *This is the most publishable piece of work in the project.*
4. **Novel algorithm:** GOP → Personal Progress Index (D+G) — the baseline-relative normalisation is the ethical contribution.
5. **Novel algorithm/system:** the bias-guarded rubric + its invariance test (H) — an auditable fairness property, not just a prompt.

Everything else is applied engineering on pretrained models. **This is the correct ratio.** A 2–3 person team that tries to train an ASR model from scratch will ship nothing.

### 9.4 Evaluation discipline (the startup-quality bit)

#### M1 · Identity, Consent & the Communication Ability Profile `[CP]` `[MVP]` — T1+T2, 1.5 weeks

**Purpose.** Build the onboarding flow that produces the CAP, and the consent system that gates everything.

**Why it matters.** The CAP is the input to the Modality Router, the recommender, the scoring engine, and the dashboards. Every other module reads it.

**Depends on.** M0.

**The onboarding paradox and how we solve it.**
> You cannot ask a user to configure accessibility using an interface that is not yet accessible to them.

Solution — a three-stage funnel that requires progressively more from the user:
1. **Stage 0 — Zero-input detection.** Read `prefers-reduced-motion`, `prefers-contrast`, `prefers-color-scheme`, screen-reader heuristics, viewport, pointer type. Apply immediately.
2. **Stage 1 — The four-door screen.** A single screen with four huge, high-contrast, self-narrating targets, each simultaneously labelled in text, pictograph, audio (auto-narrated on load), and ISL clip: *"I will listen" / "I will read" / "I will use signs" / "I will use pictures"*. Any one of them is reachable by tap, keyboard, switch-scan, or voice. **This screen is the hardest UI in the product. Budget real time for it.**
3. **Stage 2 — Channel confirmation, in the chosen modality.** Now that we have a working channel, ask the remaining questions *through it*: Can you speak? Do you want captions? How fast should audio be? How long is a comfortable session? Do you use a switch?
4. **Stage 3 (optional, later) — Speech enrolment.** 30 phrases, only if the user says they will use voice. Feeds M8. Skippable and resumable — never a wall.

**Deliverables.** Supabase Auth (email, magic link, **anonymous guest mode**), guardian-managed accounts, the funnel above, CAP CRUD + versioning, a granular consent ledger (per-purpose, per-item, revocable), and a "change how the app talks to me" settings screen reachable from everywhere in ≤2 actions.

**Technical spec.**
- CAP is versioned; changing it creates a new row, never an update. Progress data references the CAP version it was collected under.
- Consent purposes are separate rows: `basic_use`, `speech_processing`, `store_audio_for_review`, `research_corpus`, `trainer_visibility`, `institution_analytics`, `camera_on_device`. Each independently revocable. Revoking `research_corpus` triggers deletion.
- Guest mode stores CAP in IndexedDB and offers migration on signup.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- All five personas complete onboarding **unassisted** in under 4 minutes (measured in the persona walkthrough test).
- Screen-reader-only run: complete onboarding with the monitor off.
- Keyboard-only and switch-scan (2-switch) runs both complete.
- Revoking `research_corpus` verifiably deletes the stored audio (integration test).

**Risks.** Stage 1 is genuinely hard design work. Prototype it with paper/Figma and test with an actual special educator before coding.

#### M2 · The Modality Router & Accessible Design System `[CP]` `[MVP]` ★ — T1, 3 weeks

**Purpose.** Build the architectural heart: the layer that turns a `ContentBlock` + a CAP into a rendered, interactive experience.

**Why it matters.** This is Idea 1. It is the reason the project is not "another interview prep app". Everything after M2 is cheap; M2 is expensive. **Do not compress this module.**

**Depends on.** M0 (contracts), M1 (CAP).

**Technical spec.**

*Output side — the renderer registry.*
```ts
type OutputChannel = 'audio' | 'captioned_text' | 'isl' | 'pictograph' | 'easy_read';

// A renderer knows how to present ONE ContentBlock kind through ONE channel.
type Renderer = (block: ContentBlock, cap: CAP) => ReactNode;
const registry: Record<ContentBlock['kind'], Partial<Record<OutputChannel, Renderer>>>;

// The router composes: primary channel + any number of simultaneous support channels.
// A Deaf user may get captioned_text (primary) + isl (support).
// A user with ID may get easy_read (primary) + pictograph + audio (all simultaneous).
<ModalityRouter block={block} />   // reads CAP from context. Callers never choose a channel.
```
> **The rule that makes this work:** feature code may *never* import a renderer directly. It renders `<ModalityRouter block={…}/>`. An ESLint rule enforces this. This is what prevents accessibility from decaying over 6 months.

*Input side — the input adapter registry.*
Each of `SpeechInput`, `TextInput`, `AACBoardInput`, `SignInput`, `SwitchScanInput` implements one interface and emits a `LearnerResponse` with a `canonical_text`. Feature code renders `<ModalityInput block={block} onResponse={…}/>` and does not know or care which one appeared.

*Fallback chain.* If a representation is missing, degrade deterministically and **visibly log it**: `isl → captioned_text → easy_read`, `pictograph → easy_read → canonical_text`, `audio_slow → audio_native (rate-adjusted via Web Audio playbackRate)`. Missing representations are a build-time CI warning, not a runtime surprise.

*Design system (built alongside).*
- Design tokens with **two contrast themes** (standard, high-contrast) × light/dark, all verified ≥7:1 for body text.
- Minimum target size 44×44 CSS px, configurable up to 88px for motor impairment.
- A global `<Announcer/>` for `aria-live` messages — one place, correct politeness levels.
- Focus management utilities: focus trap, restore-on-close, skip links, roving tabindex.
- **Switch-scan driver**: a global scanning controller (row/column or linear scan) with configurable dwell time, driven by 1 or 2 switches mapped to keys. This is the accessibility feature everyone forgets; it is what makes P3 possible.
- Reduced-motion respected globally; no animation carries information.
- Easy-Read text component enforcing: ≤15 words/sentence, one clause per line, sans-serif ≥18px, 1.5 line height, image beside each idea.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- One fixture `ContentBlock` renders correctly and *usably* through all five output channels — demonstrated by a single Storybook page with a CAP switcher. **This screenshot is your pitch deck's best slide.**
- `axe-core` in CI: **zero** critical/serious violations across all channel combinations.
- Playwright tests drive a full lesson: keyboard-only, screen-reader-mode, and switch-scan (simulated).
- ESLint rule fails a PR that imports a renderer outside `src/modality/`.

**Risks.** Scope creep into "a component library". Build only what a lesson needs. Ruthlessly.

#### M6 · Speech Analysis Pipeline v1 — ASR, Alignment, GOP `[CP]` `[MVP]` `[ML]` ★ — T3, 3 weeks

**Purpose.** The pronunciation-assessment core: audio + known target text → per-phoneme pronunciation scores.

**Depends on.** M5.

**Pipeline.**
```
audio(16k) → preprocess (normalise, denoise-light, VAD)
           → ASR (Whisper)                       → transcript + confidence
           → G2P(target_text)                    → expected phoneme sequence
           → forced alignment (MFA / CTC-seg)    → phoneme time boundaries
           → acoustic posteriors (wav2vec2/WavLM)
           → GOP per phoneme                     → phrase-level pronunciation score
           → { intelligibility, per-phoneme detail, problem phonemes }
```

**GOP definition to implement** (state it explicitly so it's reviewable):
`GOP(p) = log P(p | O_p) − max_{q∈Q} log P(q | O_p)`, computed over the aligned frames `O_p`, then length-normalised. Aggregate to word and phrase level with a duration-weighted mean, and surface the **worst three phonemes** as the coaching target.

**Critical design decisions.**
- **Two-track ASR:** a *free* recognition pass (what did they actually say → intelligibility) and a *forced* pass against the target text (how well did they say the intended thing → GOP). Both are needed; conflating them is the classic bug.
- **Never surface raw GOP to a learner.** It is a fairness hazard. GOP goes into M7's PPI normalisation, and only the PPI is shown.
- Model versions are recorded on every attempt (`speech_attempts.model_versions`) so scores remain interpretable after a model upgrade.

**The evaluation harness (build this before the pipeline, not after).**
`services/speech/eval/` — a CLI that runs the pipeline over a frozen set (typical + atypical speech) and emits a metrics table. Wire it into CI as a regression gate. **Every subsequent speech PR must include the before/after table.**

**Acceptance criteria.** End-to-end latency < 6s for a 5-second utterance on the free CPU host; GOP correlates ρ≥0.6 with expert ratings on the labelled subset; the eval harness runs in CI; per-speaker results are reported separately for atypical speakers.

**Risks.** MFA is heavy and fiddly to containerise. **Fallback ready:** `torchaudio` CTC forced alignment — lighter, easier to deploy, slightly less precise. Decide by a spike in Week 6, record as an ADR.

#### M7 · Prosody, Disfluency & the Personal Progress Index `[CP]` `[MVP]` `[ML]` ★ — T3, 3 weeks

**Purpose.** Fluency analytics, the trained disfluency model, and the baseline-relative scoring that is Idea 2.

**Depends on.** M6.

**Part A — Prosody (deterministic).** Speech rate (words/min), articulation rate (excluding pauses), pause count/mean/distribution, longest pause, F0 mean/range/variance, energy variance, voiced ratio. Tools: Parselmouth (Praat), librosa, openSMILE eGeMAPS feature set.

**Part B — Disfluency classifier `[ML]` (trained).**
- Task: multi-label over ~3s windows → {block, prolongation, sound-repetition, word-repetition, interjection, no-disfluency}.
- Data: **SEP-28k** (primary training), **FluencyBank** (eval).
- Approach A (start here): openSMILE eGeMAPS features → LightGBM. Fast, CPU-friendly, interpretable, trains in minutes on Colab.
- Approach B (if A underperforms): frozen wav2vec2 encoder → small attention head, fine-tuned. Needs a GPU session.
- Ship as ONNX. Track both in MLflow. Target macro-F1 ≥ 0.65.

**Part C — the Personal Progress Index ★.** This is the module's reason for existing.

```
Dimensions: intelligibility · pronunciation · pace · fluency · confidence

Baseline: for each dimension d, maintain a rolling EWMA mean μ_d and std σ_d over the
          user's own attempts. Bootstrapped from the first N=10 attempts ("calibration
          period" — show progress qualitatively, not numerically, during it).

PPI_d(t) = clamp( 50 + 15 * (x_d(t) − μ_d) / max(σ_d, ε), 0, 100 )
μ_d, σ_d ← EWMA update with α tuned so the baseline tracks over ~3 weeks, not 3 days.

Composite PPI = CAP-weighted mean of dimensions.
  → For a profile with a stammer, `fluency` weight is reduced and `intelligibility`/
    `content` weight is raised. The weighting is stored on the CAP and is visible to
    the learner and trainer. It is never hidden.
```

**Non-negotiable rules, enforced by tests:**
- R1. No output of this module may reference a non-disabled reference speaker.
- R2. A detected disfluency event emits a **coaching cue object** (`{event, timestamp, suggested_strategy}`), never a score deduction. Strategies come from a fixed, SLP-reviewed library: easy onset, light contact, pull-out, pausing, breath support.
- R3. The baseline must be inspectable: the learner can always see "your baseline for pace was 92 wpm, today you were 104 wpm".
- R4. During the calibration period, show *no* numeric score at all. Show "we're learning how you speak."

**Acceptance criteria.**
- Disfluency model hits target macro-F1 and is exported to ONNX.
- **Monotonicity test:** synthetic sequences of improving attempts produce a monotonically rising PPI.
- **Fairness test:** two synthetic speakers — one typical, one with injected disfluency but identical content and improvement trajectory — produce *statistically indistinguishable PPI trajectories*. This test is the proof of Idea 2. Put it in CI.
- Every disfluency event has an attached coaching cue; assert no code path converts one into a deduction.

#### M8 · Personalised ASR Adaptation `[ML]` ★ — T3, 3 weeks

**Purpose.** Make ASR actually work for atypical speech. This is the most technically novel module and the strongest research contribution.

**Why it matters.** If ASR fails P3 (dysarthria), the entire speech half of the product fails him. Base Whisper WER on dysarthric speech can exceed 50–70%. That is unusable.

**Depends on.** M1 (enrolment flow), M6.

**Technical spec.**
1. **Enrolment set:** 30 phrases chosen for phonetic coverage (drawn from the phrase bank so enrolment is itself useful practice, not a chore). Resumable across sessions.
2. **Speaker embedding:** SpeechBrain ECAPA-TDNN x-vector from enrolment audio. Stored on `speaker_profiles`.
3. **Adaptation, in escalating order of cost — implement in this order and stop when good enough:**
   - **(a) Prompt/vocabulary biasing** — constrain decoding with the expected phrase set. Nearly free, surprisingly effective for our closed-vocabulary drills. **Do this first.**
   - **(b) Shallow fusion / n-gram biasing** toward the phrase bank.
   - **(c) LoRA adapter fine-tune** of Whisper's encoder/decoder on the 30 enrolment clips (heavy augmentation: speed, pitch, noise, SpecAugment). ~2–5MB per user, trained on Colab or a cheap serverless GPU, cached in object storage, loaded per-request.
   - **(d) `[V2]` Pooled adaptation** — cluster users by speech characteristics; a new user starts from their cluster's adapter, so cold-start improves before any personal training.
4. **Guardrails:** never regress. Always evaluate the adapted model against the base on the user's own held-out clips; if it is worse, do not deploy it. Store `wer_before`/`wer_after` on the profile and **show it to the learner** — "the app now understands you 34% better" is a delightful moment and a great demo beat.
5. Retraining trigger: every N new confirmed transcriptions, or on user request.

**Acceptance criteria.** ≥25% relative WER reduction on held-out atypical speech (TORGO/UASpeech), **reported per speaker**; adapter train time < 15 min on a free Colab T4; per-request adapter load adds < 500ms; no user's WER gets worse than base.

**Risks.** Dataset access delays (see 9.3 — apply Week 1). Free-tier GPU limits. **Mitigation:** stage (a) alone delivers a real, demoable improvement with zero GPU. Ship (a) early; (c) is the upgrade.

#### M9 · GenAI Core — RAG, Guardrails & Scenario Role-Play `[CP]` `[MVP]` ★ — T2, 3.5 weeks

**Purpose.** Live, multi-turn workplace role-play with an AI colleague, grounded in our corpus and incapable of going off the rails.

**Depends on.** M3 (corpus), M2 (rendering).

**Architecture.**
```
turn request
  → build context: CAP + scenario state + learner's recent error signature + target phrases
  → RAG retrieve: top-k ContentBlocks from pgvector (filtered by scenario tags & difficulty)
  → LLM call: system prompt (versioned, hashed) + schema-constrained response
  → guardrail chain: schema validate → vocabulary check → scope check → safety check
                   → readability check (matched to CAP text_complexity)
  → on failure: repair-retry once, then fall back to a scripted turn
  → emit ContentBlock(kind='scenario_turn') → Modality Router renders it
```

**The LLM response schema** (constrained output — never free text):
```jsonc
{
  "npc_utterance": "string",           // what the AI colleague says
  "npc_intent": "enum",
  "expected_learner_intents": ["…"],   // what a good reply would do
  "target_phrases": ["phrase.id", …],  // from OUR bank — this is the grounding
  "difficulty_delta": -1 | 0 | +1,     // ZPD adjustment
  "scaffold": {                         // shown if the learner struggles
    "hint": "string",
    "sentence_starter": "string",
    "choices": ["…"]                   // becomes the AAC/multiple-choice path
  },
  "easy_read_version": "string",
  "scenario_state": { "…" }
}
```
> `scaffold.choices` is what makes role-play work for P4 (intellectual disability) and P3 (motor). The same LLM turn becomes free-form conversation for one user and a 3-choice tap for another. **One generation, five renderings.** This is the Modality Router paying off.

**Scenario library (author 12–15, each with 3 difficulty tiers):** first day introduction · asking a supervisor to repeat · reporting a delay · requesting leave · reporting a safety issue · handling critical feedback · joining a standup · a customer/client call · disagreeing with a teammate · asking for an accommodation · a phone call to HR · lunchroom small talk.

**Difficulty adaptation (ZPD).** Track a rolling success rate. Target 70–80% success. Difficulty levers: vocabulary tier, sentence length, turn length, interruption/noise in the scenario, scaffold availability, whether the NPC is patient or brisk. **Never speed. Never a timer.**

**Guardrails — non-negotiable.**
- JSON-schema validation on every response; malformed → repair-retry → scripted fallback.
- Vocabulary constraint: `npc_utterance` must be within the difficulty tier's word list + the scenario's allowed terms.
- Scope check: reject any turn that leaves the workplace scenario.
- Safety: toxicity/harassment filter; plus a **disability-condescension filter** — a rubric-scored check that the NPC is not being patronising. Ship a fixture set of condescending phrasings that must be rejected.
- Provider behind an interface (`LLMProvider`) with Claude primary and a free-tier model as dev/fallback. Prompts are files, versioned, hash-recorded with every generation.

**Cost control.** Cache aggressively (identical context → cached turn), keep outputs short, use a cheaper model for classification/routing sub-calls, and enforce a per-user daily token budget.

**Acceptance criteria.** 20-turn role-play completes without a guardrail breach on an adversarial fixture set; every turn renders in all five modalities; a scripted-fallback path is exercised by test (simulate LLM outage); p95 turn latency < 3s; the condescension fixture set is 100% rejected.

#### M10 · Social Story Generator `[MVP]` — T2, 1.5 weeks

**Purpose.** Generate personalised social stories for the learner's actual job context, rendered quad-modally.

**Depends on.** M9.

**Technical spec.**
- Follow the standard **Carol Gray social-story structure**: descriptive, perspective, directive, and affirmative sentences, with a ratio of at least 2:1 descriptive-to-directive. Encode this as a schema constraint, not a prompt suggestion.
- Input: job context (from CAP + a short form), the specific situation, the learner's reading level.
- Output: 6–10 panels; each panel = one idea, one sentence, one pictograph, one audio narration, optionally one ISL clip.
- **Human-in-the-loop:** stories are `draft` until a trainer approves them, if the learner is linked to a trainer. Unlinked learners get them immediately with a clear "AI-generated" label.
- Library: save, re-read, and share stories; pre-seed 10 hand-written exemplars so the feature is non-empty on day one and so the LLM has few-shot examples.

**Acceptance criteria.** Generated stories pass the structural validator (sentence-type ratio, ≤1 idea per panel, reading level); render as Easy-Read + pictograph strip + narrated audio simultaneously; trainer approval flow works.

#### M11 · Mock Interview, HR Simulator & the Bias-Guarded Rubric `[CP]` `[MVP]` `[ML]` ★★ — T2+T3, 4 weeks

**Purpose.** The flagship feature and the fairness centrepiece.

**Depends on.** M9, M7.

**Sub-features.**

*11.1 Interview simulator.* Three tracks — **HR/behavioural**, **role/technical**, **telephonic** (audio-only, no visual cues, deliberately harder). 8–12 questions, adaptive follow-ups ("you mentioned X — tell me more"). Configurable interviewer persona: *supportive* / *neutral* / *brisk* — and the learner chooses, with `supportive` as default. Full transcript + replay of their own answers.

*11.2 The bias-guarded rubric ★★.*

```
SCORED DIMENSIONS (the rubric may grade these):
  content_relevance     — did the answer address the question?
  structure_star        — situation/task/action/result present?
  specificity           — concrete examples vs. generic claims?
  clarity_of_intent     — is the point recoverable? (NOT: is it well-articulated?)
  self_advocacy         — did they present their strengths and needs?
  role_alignment        — does the answer fit the job?

EXCLUDED DIMENSIONS (the rubric MUST NOT grade these — enforced, not requested):
  speech_rate · articulation_quality · fluency/disfluency · accent · voice_quality
  gaze/eye_contact · facial_affect · body_posture · motor_stillness · response_latency
  grammatical_perfection · vocabulary_sophistication
```

**How the exclusion is *enforced* rather than merely instructed** — four independent layers:
1. **Input scrubbing.** The rubric scorer receives a **normalised transcript only** — disfluencies removed, pauses collapsed, timing stripped. It is *architecturally blind* to the excluded traits. This is the strongest layer: you cannot penalise what you never received.
2. **Schema constraint.** The scoring response schema has exactly the six scored dimensions. There is no field for anything else.
3. **The disfluency-invariance test (CI gate).** Take N real transcripts. Programmatically inject disfluencies, fillers, repetitions, and long pauses. Score both versions. **Assert |Δscore| < ε.** If the injection changes the score, the build fails. *This is a provable fairness property, and it is the single most impressive thing in the demo.*
4. **Audit record.** Every score persists `{rubric_version, scored_dimensions, excluded_dimensions, prompt_hash, model_id, evidence_spans}`. Exportable.

*11.3 Feedback, delivered well.* Strengths first, always. Maximum **two** improvement points per session — more is demoralising and unusable. Every point is anchored to an evidence span in their own transcript ("when you said '…', adding the result would strengthen it"). Speech-related coaching (from M7) is presented **separately and explicitly labelled as optional coaching, never as interview score.**

*11.4 Accommodation & Disclosure Coach ★.* The feature no competitor has. Rehearses: whether to disclose, when to disclose, how to phrase a request, how to respond if the employer reacts badly. Includes a jurisdiction-aware **rights primer (RPwD Act 2016)** — informational, clearly not legal advice. Branching outcomes so the learner practises both the good and the bad employer response. Handle with care: this is emotionally loaded content, so include an explicit exit and a "talk to your trainer" route.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- Full 10-question interview completes in all five modalities (yes — a text-only interview for a non-verbal learner is a first-class path, not a fallback).
- **Disfluency-invariance test passes in CI** with ε defined and documented.
- Rubric agreement with 2 human raters: Cohen's κ ≥ 0.6 on 30 recorded answers.
- Audit record produced for 100% of scores; trainer override path works and is logged.
- Interview can be paused and resumed at any point (essential for P4/P5 and for anxiety).

**Risks.** LLM scoring variance. **Mitigation:** temperature 0, few-shot anchored examples per score level, self-consistency (score 3× and take the median) for the final report, and a documented κ measurement rather than an assumed one.

#### M12 · Gamification & Motivation `[MVP]` — T1, 1.5 weeks

**Purpose.** Sustain the daily habit, without the dark patterns.

**Depends on.** M4.

**Spec.** XP per completed activity (weighted by effort, **not** by score — effort is what we are rewarding). Streaks **with freeze tokens and no punishment on break** (a rigid streak is actively harmful for users with fluctuating health). Badges across four families: consistency, mastery, courage (attempted a hard scenario, practised disclosure), and growth (beat your own baseline). Achievement tiers. Weekly personal-best summary.

**Rules.** No leaderboards comparing learners to each other — Idea 2 applies to motivation design too. No loss aversion. No timers. Every badge is announced accessibly (`aria-live` + haptic + visual + audio), and badge art carries a text label.

#### M13 · Adaptive Recommendation Engine `[MVP]` — T2, 2 weeks

**Purpose.** Answer "what should I do next?" well enough that the learner never faces an empty screen.

**Depends on.** M4, M7, M9.

**v1 — transparent and rule-based** (do not start with ML here):
```
candidates = due_cards ∪ weak_phonemes ∪ failed_scenario_intents
             ∪ unattempted_next_difficulty ∪ low_confidence_self_reports

score(candidate) = w1·overdue_ness + w2·error_signature_match
                 + w3·goal_alignment (from CAP job goal)
                 + w4·variety_bonus − w5·recent_failure_penalty (protect morale)

constraints: fits session_length; ≤2 hard items per session;
             always include ≥1 item the learner is likely to succeed at
```
Every recommendation carries a **human-readable `reason_code`** shown to the learner: *"because the /r/ sound has been tricky this week."* Explainability is a product feature, not a debug tool — and it is what makes a trainer trust the system.

**v2 `[V2]`.** Contextual bandit over reason_codes once there is real telemetry.

**Acceptance criteria.** Never returns empty; respects session length; every recommendation has a displayed reason; simulated 90-day learner histories produce sensible curricula (snapshot-tested).

#### M14 · The Three Dashboards `[CP]` `[MVP]` — T1, 3 weeks

**Purpose.** Make competence visible to the learner, their trainer, and their institution.

**Depends on.** M4, M7, M11, M12.

**14.1 Learner dashboard.** PPI trend per dimension **with the baseline drawn on the chart** (this is the emotional payoff — you can see yourself rising above your own line). Phrases mastered / due / learning. Scenario and interview history with replay. Badges. Next-best-action from M13. Fully accessible charts: every chart has an equivalent **data table**, a text summary, and sonification `[V2]`.

**14.2 Trainer / special-educator dashboard.** Cohort heatmap (learners × skills). Per-learner drill-down with attempt replay. **Override AI feedback** with a reason (recorded, and used as training signal later). Assign specific phrases/scenarios/stories. Flag a learner for attention. Export a progress report as PDF. Review queue for AI-generated social stories.

> The trainer dashboard is what turns this from a consumer app into something an institution will actually deploy. It is not optional and it is not secondary. Give it real design time.

**14.3 Institution / employer dashboard.** **Consent-gated and anonymised by default** — no individual is visible unless that learner explicitly consented to institutional visibility. Cohort competency distribution, placement-readiness view, pre/post pilot outcomes, module completion funnels. Aggregation floor: **suppress any cell with n<5** to prevent re-identification.

**Acceptance criteria.** All charts have accessible equivalents; RLS verified by test (a trainer cannot read a learner outside their cohort; an institution cannot read a non-consenting learner); k-anonymity floor enforced; PDF export works.

#### M15 · Offline-First & On-Device Inference `[MVP]` — T1, 2.5 weeks

**Purpose.** Deliver the abstract's promise: it works where the learners are.

**Depends on.** M4, M6.

**Spec.**
- Service worker + Workbox: app shell, content, and the assets for the next ~50 due cards precached. Storage budget managed and shown to the user.

- Conflict resolution: last-write-wins on progress; **append-only for attempts** (never lose a recording).

Full GOP/prosody analysis is queued and runs on reconnect. Be explicit in the UI: *"Practised offline — detailed feedback will arrive when you reconnect."*
- On-device TTS via the Web Speech API where available; precached Piper audio otherwise.
- Clear, accessible offline/sync status indicator.

**Acceptance criteria.** Full drill session completes with the network disabled; queued attempts sync correctly after 24h offline; on-device ASR runs in <10s per utterance on a mid-range Android in Chrome; nothing is lost when the tab is killed mid-session.

#### M16 · ISL & AAC Depth `[ML]` — T1+T3, 3 weeks

**Purpose.** Make the sign and symbol channels genuinely first-class rather than viewer-only.

**Depends on.** M2, M3.

**16.1 AAC composition input.** A symbol board (ARASAAC/Mulberry) organised by scenario, with core-vocabulary + fringe layout, symbol→text composition, prediction, and **switch-scan navigation**. Emits a `LearnerResponse` with `canonical_text` — so AAC users flow through the *entire* product including interviews.

**16.2 ISL output.** Curated clip library (top 100 phrases + UI chrome vocabulary), a consistent player with speed control and looping, and an ISL gloss displayed alongside. `[V2]`: 3D avatar — explicitly deferred, and say why in the pitch (a bad avatar is worse than none).

**16.3 ISL recognition input `[ML]`.** MediaPipe Holistic landmarks → temporal classifier (BiLSTM/small Transformer) over a **bounded 50–100 workplace sign vocabulary**. Runs on-device (MediaPipe Tasks Web). Video never leaves the device — only the predicted label does, satisfying E4. Confidence-gated: below threshold, offer a confirmation choice rather than guessing. Train on **INCLUDE** plus self-recorded clips.

**Acceptance criteria.** P2 (Deaf, ISL-primary) completes a full scenario using sign input and ISL output; classifier ≥85% top-1 on the bounded vocabulary, reported per signer; no video frame ever leaves the client (assert by network test).

#### M17 · Privacy, Consent Enforcement & Security `[CP]` `[MVP]` — T2, 2 weeks

**Purpose.** Turn the Ethics Charter and DPDP alignment into enforced code.

**Depends on.** M1, M5.

**Spec.**
- **Data map**: every field classified (identifier / sensitive / biometric / derived), with a documented retention period. Voice is treated as biometric — highest tier.
- **Retention jobs**, running and monitored: raw audio purged at TTL; video never persisted; derived features retained per consent.
- **Consent enforcement at the query layer**, not the UI layer. A middleware that refuses to serve data whose purpose-consent is absent. UI-level consent checks are a bug waiting to happen.
- **Row-level security** on every table; deny-by-default; policies tested.
- **Data subject rights**: full export (JSON + human-readable PDF) and full erasure, both self-service, both completing within the statutory window, both tested end-to-end.
- **Guardian mode**: for learners under 18 or under guardianship, consent flows through a linked guardian with a recorded evidence trail.
- Standard security hygiene: rate limiting, input validation, signed URLs with short expiry, dependency scanning, secret scanning, security headers/CSP, audit log append-only.

**Acceptance criteria.** Erasure test proves every artefact including object storage is gone; a consent-revocation integration test proves data becomes unservable immediately; RLS penetration tests pass; the data map is published in `docs/`.

#### M18 · Accessibility Validation & Pilot `[CP]` — T1+T2+T3, 3 weeks + ongoing

**Purpose.** Prove the central claim with evidence, not assertion.

**Depends on.** everything.

**18.1 Automated.** `axe-core` on every route × every modality combination in CI. Lighthouse a11y ≥ 95. Contrast tokens verified programmatically. Keyboard-trap detection.

**18.2 Manual — the part that actually counts.** Screen-reader passes with **NVDA (Windows)**, **VoiceOver (macOS/iOS)**, and **TalkBack (Android)** — real passes, monitor off, on the top 10 flows. **Acceptance criteria.** Zero critical/serious axe violations; all three screen readers pass the top 10 flows; the persona suite is green; pilot targets met (see Section 16).

## 13. Definition of Done — the startup-quality bar

A module is done when **all** of the following are true. Not most.

- [ ] Works for all five personas, verified by the persona walkthrough suite
- [ ] Zero critical/serious `axe-core` violations
- [ ] Keyboard-only **and** screen-reader **and** switch-scan paths verified
- [ ] Works offline, or degrades with a clear, accessible message
- [ ] Unit tests for logic; one Playwright E2E for the happy path
- [ ] `[ML]` modules: eval table in the PR, regression gate in CI, model version recorded
- [ ] Errors are handled and surfaced accessibly — no silent failures, no raw stack traces
- [ ] Loading, empty, error, and offline states all designed (not just the happy path)
- [ ] Consent and retention enforced at the query layer for any new data
- [ ] Copy reviewed for dignity: no infantilising language, no "special", no pity framing
- [ ] Instrumented — the events needed to know if it works in the pilot exist
- [ ] Documented in `docs/`, and an ADR written if a decision was made

## 18. The one-paragraph version, for when someone asks

> SAMVAAD is a workplace-communication trainer for persons with disabilities, built on two ideas that no existing product has. First, accessibility is architecture: every lesson is stored as modality-neutral data and rendered at runtime through whatever channels the learner can actually use — speech, text, Indian Sign Language, picture symbols, or Easy-Read — so it is structurally impossible to ship a lesson that excludes someone. Second, scoring is baseline-relative: we measure each learner against their own rolling baseline instead of against a non-disabled reference speaker, and our mock-interview rubric is architecturally blind to speech rate, articulation, gaze, and affect — a fairness property we prove with an automated invariance test in CI, not one we merely claim. On top of that: 226 curated workplace phrases with spaced repetition, RAG-grounded generative role-play with an AI recruiter or manager, auto-generated social stories, a full mock-interview simulator with an accommodation-disclosure coach, and dashboards for learners, trainers, and institutions. It runs offline on entry-level devices, keeps voice and video on-device, and is built to be reviewed and overridden by the human special educator it exists to multiply.
